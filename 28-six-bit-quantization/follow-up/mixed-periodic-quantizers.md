# Two possibly different templates with a common 32-element period

On the uniform Boolean 12-cube, let L be any five-dimensional binary linear subspace. Suppose the 64 cells of a **balanced** quantizer are A+ℓ and A′+ℓ for ℓ∈L, all disjoint, each of size 64. A and A′ may have unrelated shapes and coordinate orientations. Their average squared-bias coefficient is at most six, with equality precisely if both templates are coordinate six-faces (not necessarily with the same orientation). For 0≤1−2p≤1/1024 this gives the six-coordinate mutual-information bound; away from 1/2 in this interval it is strict unless both are faces.

This is a different restriction than [the one-template theorem](translational-quantizers.md): we allow genuinely different cell shapes, but here the common period must be linear and have dimension five. Arbitrary 64-cell partitions and four-orbit constructions with period dimension four are not covered.

**Near-threshold rigidity.** Use H=4096B and the weighted top-64 comparator A* from the one-template proof. Among the 1,027,170 shifted families, precisely 49 have H≥24,480: one six-face, 24 with maximum weight two, and 24 with just one point of weight greater than two. Every nonface representative has every sign sum at least 38. This is exhaustively checked by the same enumeration with its threshold lowered by 96, followed by `near-summary.py`. The maximum over all 64-point sets remains 24,672.

We claim that any A with H≥24,480 is either a face or has at most one point outside the radius-two ball around its coordinate-majority vertex. Translate this vertex to zero and sort the coordinates as before. If A* is the face, then ||s(A)−s(A*)||²≤96. In particular the six free coordinates (where s(A*)ᵢ=0) contribute at most 96 to H(A). Unless A is that face, at least one of the other sign sums is at most 62 rather than 64, costing at least 252. Its total deficit from 24,576 is then at least 156, a contradiction. Otherwise ||s(A)−s(A*)||²≤192, so each s(A)ᵢ≥38−√192>24 and is even, hence at least 26. If A is not a downset, move an upper word across a downward edge to its absent lower neighbor. This increases H by at least 4·26+4=108 and leaves the other sign sums unchanged. There cannot be two such moves (the total slack is at most 192); after the first one, H≥24,588>24,576. By the strict rigidity theorem, the resulting downset contains no word of weight higher than two: its majority vertex is still zero. Undoing the one move proves the claim in this case.

If A is already a downset, pair shifts preserve layers. If its shifted representative is a nonface, the enumerated layer bound applies. If a true nonpermutation shift ever leads eventually to a coordinate face, take the last such shift. Its image is a face up to the subsequent permutations. An inverse nonpermutation shift of a face must split the 32 exclusive fibers of one free and one fixed coordinate into t and 32−t, 1≤t≤31; this loses at least 8t(32−t)≥248 units of H. (Two free or two fixed coordinates have no exclusive fibers.) Hence a nonface downset that compresses to a face has H≤24,576−248<24,480, also impossible.

**Covering fact.** A six-dimensional affine subspace of the 12-cube leaves at least 64 cube vertices outside the radius-two balls around its members. In syndrome language, some coset of the underlying linear code has no representative of weight ≤2. For a standalone exhaustive check, normalize six independent columns of a parity-check matrix to the unit vectors of F₂⁶. The remaining six columns can be assumed distinct, nonzero and different from the units: replacing repetitions or zeros by unused columns can only enlarge the set of syndromes obtainable as 0, a single column or a sum of two columns. Enumerate all binomial(57,6)=36,288,252 ways to complete the units, and count these syndromes using a 64-bit mask. Their maximum count is 63, not 64 (`affine-cover.cpp`). This is also the known radius bound for binary [12,6] codes in [Baicheva and Bouyukliev, Table 1](https://www.moi.math.bas.bg/moiuser/~iliya/pdf_site/pdfTc_Il_AMC.pdf). We do not claim the coding theorem as new.

**The partition.** If the average H exceeds 24,576, one template has H>24,576 and the other has H>24,480 by the universal maximum. For equality either the same alternative holds with the second inequality weak, or both H equal 24,576. When neither template is a face, their majority vertices and the centers obtained by translating them by L form two cosets of L. Their union lies in an affine subspace of dimension at most six, so at least 64 vertices lie beyond both radius-two systems. At least one orbit consists entirely of cells with H≥24,576, all lying inside their respective balls by the one-template paper's individual-cell rigidity. The other orbit has at most one point per cell outside its own ball, only 32 points altogether, a contradiction. If neither template exceeds the threshold but equality holds, both are nonface level cells or faces; the same argument covers the former.

If one template is a face, its 32 disjoint translates cover an affine subspace of size 2048 (the sum of the six free-coordinate directions and L, then translated). The other template is contained in the complementary affine hyperplane. Every nonface with H≥24,576 contains a majority vertex and all its 12 single-bit neighbors and so has full affine span; it cannot be the other template. Therefore equality requires two faces and strict excess is impossible. Two faces attain the benchmark for every crossover.

For a non-equality case the average coefficient falls short of six by at least 1/2048, since each H is a multiple of four. The explicit divergence bound from the one-template proof, per cell, is Bρ²/2+117ρ⁴ in nats. The same constants work here even for B slightly above six, using B≤24672/4096: E L⁴≤3B²<(13/4)⁴. Averaging and using 117/1024²<1/4096 yields the stated noise interval.
