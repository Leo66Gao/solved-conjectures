# The critical ball and edge-ball types cannot tile the ten-cube

**Theorem.** Suppose a partition of \(\{0,1\}^{10}\) into exactly 64 cells uses only radius-one balls in ten dimensions (11 points), products of a free bit and a radius-one ball in nine dimensions (20 points), and coordinate four-faces (16 points). Then every cell is a coordinate four-face.

Write \(A,B,F\) for these shape types; all translated and coordinate-permuted copies are allowed. A \(B\)-tile is the union of the two full radius-one balls centered at the two ends of the edge in its free direction. With exactly 64 cells, the volume equation gives counts \((4q,5q,64-9q)\) with integer \(0\le q\le7\).

## Exact shell restrictions

The integer certificates in **tiling-duals.json** and their independent standard-library verifier **tiling-obstruction.py** prove four restrictions:
1. If \(q>0\), then \(q<6\).
2. An \(A\)-center and a \(B\)-center edge have minimum distance in \(\{3,4,5,6\}\); if \(q=5\), that distance must be 4.
3. Two \(A\)-centers cannot have distance at least 8.
4. Edges of two \(B\)-tiles having distinct free directions must be at minimum distance 3, measured outside those two directions.

The automatic lower bound 3 in (2) and (4) follows from disjointness of radius-one balls about the edges' endpoints. For the upper restrictions, fix one or two tiles in the claimed relative positions. Translate and permute them to canonical positions. Partition all coordinates into blocks by their fixed values and marked axes in the fixed tiles. Use as rows the orbits of cube words under permutations within each block: a word's row is its vector of block weights. For a remaining eligible tile, its column contains the count of its points on each remaining row, plus an entry 1 in its type row. The rows of the right-hand side are uncovered word counts, followed by the three remaining tile counts with the \(q\)-dependent terms moved to a separate column. In particular the \(q\)-column is \((-4,-5,9)\) on type rows and zero elsewhere. A partition produces a nonnegative solution \(M v=b\) with last variable \(q\).

The stored integer vectors \(y\) and positive integers \(s\) verify either (i) \(y^TM\le (0,\dots,0,-s)\), hence \(q\le-y^Tb/s\), or (ii) \(y^TM\le0\) and \(y^Tb>0\), which is impossible. All candidates are enumerated by the verifier rather than trusting a floating-point LP. It checks canonical fixed tile positions, label coverage, and all inequalities independently. To justify canonicalization for two different edge directions, choose directions 1 and 2, translate their axes independently to make the specified nonfree endpoints zero on both marked coordinates, and permute the remaining coordinates so the common outside-axis difference uses the initial block. The analogous canonicalizations for an \(A\)-center and an edge, and for two \(A\)-centers, are immediate. The log records the certified bounds. For (1) the rational certificate gives \(q\le16/3<6\).

## Fourier restrictions

Let \(a_S=\sum_c(-1)^{\sum_{i\notin S}c_i}\), summed over \(A\)-centers. Let \(b_S\) be the same character sum over \(B\)-edges whose free direction belongs to \(S\), using either endpoint as representative. Let \(b_i\) be the **number** of \(B\)-edges of direction \(i\), distinct from the signed sum \(b_{\{i\}}\). Partitioning the cube and evaluating the character outside \(S\), for \(0\le|S|\le3\), gives
\[
 (2|S|-9)a_S+(4|S|-20)b_S=0.
\]
Indeed the \(A\) multiplier is \(11-2(10-|S|)\). A \(B\)-tile contributes zero unless its free direction is excluded from the character, and then has multiplier \(2[10-2(10-|S|)]\). Every four-face contributes zero. In particular \(a_\varnothing=0\); each singleton \(a_{\{i\}}\) is a multiple of 16 and \(b_{\{i\}}\) a multiple of 7; at degrees two and three, \(a_S\) is a multiple of 12 and 8 respectively. If all \(b_i\) are even, every \(b_S\) is even, and the latter divisibilities sharpen to 24 and 16.

If \(q\le3\), there are fewer than 16 \(A\)-centers, so the singleton sums vanish and all \(b_i\) are even. The degree-two and degree-three sums also vanish. The nonzero signed function which equals ambient parity on \(A\)-centers and zero elsewhere then has all Fourier degrees through three zero, but support less than 16. This contradicts the lemma below. If \(q=5\), fix a \(B\)-edge of direction \(i\). By (2), every \(A\)-center projected outside \(i\) has distance exactly 4 to that edge, so their projected parities all agree. Then \(a_{\{i\}}=\pm20\), not a multiple of 16. Only \(q=4\) remains.

## A minimal-support lemma

If a nonzero real function on a Boolean cube has zero Fourier coefficients in degrees below \(r\), its support has at least \(2^r\) points. In the equality case the support consists of \(r\) independent choices of antipodal configurations on disjoint nonempty coordinate blocks, with the other coordinates fixed, and the values on that support equal a nonzero constant times the parity of the \(r\) block choices.

Proof by induction on ambient dimension. The \(r=0\) equality case is a singleton. Otherwise choose a varying coordinate and form the two restrictions \(f_0,f_1\), both nonzero with no degrees below \(r-1\), so each support has size at least \(2^{r-1}\). If \(f_0+f_1=0\), their supports agree and opposite values add one independent singleton block. Otherwise that sum has no degrees below \(r\), so by induction it has support at least \(2^r\). Equality forces the restrictions to have disjoint supports and their sum has the claimed form on the projection. The Boolean indicator of which projected support point belongs to \(f_1\), as a function of the \(r\) block choices, has no Fourier degrees above one: testing \(f_1\) against a block monomial of degree at most \(r-2\) is accomplished by picking one original coordinate from each indicated block, and multiplication by the projected parity interchanges that degree with its complement. A Boolean function of degree at most one that takes both values is a coordinate or its complement (square the affine \(\{\pm1\}\)-representation and compare coefficients). The lifted coordinate therefore joins one existing block, proving the claim.

## Eliminating \(q=4\)

There are 16 \(A\)-centers and 20 \(B\)-edges. Let \(T\) be the set of directions with odd \(b_i\). Each nonzero \(a_{\{i\}}\) has magnitude 16 and corresponds exactly to \(i\in T\), with \(|b_{\{i\}}|=7\). Thus \(|T|\le2\), and \(|T|\) is even. Suppose \(T\) consists of directions \(i,j\). By (4), every pair of projected \(B_i\)- and \(B_j\)-centers outside those directions is at distance 3. For projected \(B_i\)-centers, the outside-\(\{i,j\}\) parity is therefore constant, and the imbalance \(|b_{\{i\}}|=7\) implies at least seven \(B_i\)-centers have the same bit \(j\). Translate so one projected \(B_j\)-center is zero. These seven projected \(B_i\)-centers are then weight-three triples on eight coordinates; because their bit \(j\) agrees and their parallel edge tiles are disjoint, the triples meet pairwise in at most one point. Any other projected \(B_j\)-center with support \(Z\) must satisfy \(|S\cap Z|=|Z|/2\) for each of the seven triples \(S\). If \(|Z|=2\), at most six triples can meet exactly one of its points (each point lies in at most three triples). If \(|Z|=4\), at most six triples can use a distinct pair in \(Z\). If \(|Z|=6\), at most five triples are contained in \(Z\), by counting pairs. Weight eight is impossible. Thus only \(Z=0\) is possible, and the projection of the \(B_j\)-centers is injective because dropping coordinate \(i\) cannot identify two disjoint parallel edges. This contradicts \(b_j\ge7\). So \(T\) is empty, all orientation counts are even, and each nonempty orientation is equally split by the parity of its center outside the free coordinate.

Let \(s\) be the number of nonempty directions; their coordinate set is \(D\). For \(i\ne j\) in \(D\), condition (4) says every pair of their edges has outside-\(\{i,j\}\) distance three. In each direction \(i\), the bit in direction \(j\) is therefore determined by the center's parity \(\alpha\) outside \(i\): the outside-\(\{i,j\}\) parity is fixed, as can be seen by comparing with one fixed \(B_j\)-center. For any \(i\) and parity class \(\alpha\), only inactive coordinates (outside \(D\)) vary among edges of that class. Their inactive strings have the same parity and have pairwise distance at least four (parallel edge-tile disjointness). For two different directions \(i,j\), a fixed parity class in \(i\) sees constant inactive distances \(d_0,d_1\) to the two respective parity classes in \(j\), and \(d_0+d_1=8-s\). The reason is that switching parity in \(j\) complements all of its bits on the \(s-2\) shared active coordinates, while each total outside-\(\{i,j\}\) distance is three.

- If \(s\ge5\), at least one of \(d_0,d_1\) is at most one, so a fixed point of that other class can see at most one point of the first class (distance-four separation). Every class is a singleton and \(s\le8\) because both distances are nonnegative. This gives at most 16 edges.
- If \(s=4\), any nonsingleton class requires both cross distances to other directions to be at least two, hence both are two. Such a class has at most three points: viewed from any other-class point, the distance-two changes are pairwise disjoint subsets of the six inactive coordinates. If a class has three points, these three pairs partition the six coordinates, and their only common distance-two neighbor is the original reference point. Indeed another neighbor with support \(Z\) would have \(|S\cap Z|=|Z|/2\) for each of the three disjoint pairs, forcing \(Z=\varnothing\). All other direction classes are then singleton, so there are at most \(6+3\cdot2=12\) edges. If no class has size three, there are at most 16.
- If \(s=3\), a nonsingleton class requires both distances at least two, and one is two since they sum to five. Hence a class has at most three points (disjoint pairs on seven coordinates), giving at most 18 edges.

Thus \(s=1\) or \(2\). Project the \(A\)-centers onto the inactive coordinates (outside \(D\)). This projection is injective because distinct original centers are at distance at least three. Retain their signed ambient parities as values of a function on the projected cube. Its Fourier coefficients through degree two vanish, by the bounds above, and its degree-three coefficients vanish since their index sets are outside \(D\), so \(b_S=0\). By the minimal-support lemma its 16 positions form a four-block cube with alternating values.

If \(s=1\), every block must have size at least two or two original centers would be at distance at most two. Opposite corners in the projected block cube therefore have distance at least eight, contradicting (3). If \(s=2\), a block of size one requires both omitted bits to flip whenever that block is toggled, for distance at least three. Two size-one blocks would then produce a pair of original centers at distance two by toggling them together. If all four block sizes are at least two, their opposite corners are again forbidden. The only other possibility with projected distance less than eight has sizes \(1,2,2,2\), hence projected distance seven. Opposite projected corners have the same alternating sign (four blocks change), so their ambient parities agree; the two omitted bits must make an odd contribution to distance, giving total distance eight. This is again forbidden and completes the proof.

This result rules out realizing the two-cell affine obstruction with only its critical geometric shapes and coordinate four-faces. It does not itself prove anything for other cell types, and a ten-input counterexample using different cell types has not been ruled out.
