# A sharp six-orbit result for the Erdős–Gyárfás power-of-two cycle problem

**Research note, 26 November 2026.** Computer-assisted theorem; no claim of a solution to unrestricted Erdős Problem 64, external peer review, or Lean formalization.

## Statement

Let a finite abelian group A act by automorphisms on a nonempty finite simple graph G of minimum degree at least three. Assume the action is **semiregular**: no nonidentity element fixes any vertex. Write r for the number of vertex orbits.

1. If r ≤ 5, G contains a simple cycle of length 4, 8, or 16.
2. If r = 6, the same conclusion holds with 32 added. If G contains none of the first three lengths, it contains an A-invariant spanning subgraph that is an abelian lift of the simple graph K₃,₃ (one A-orbit of edges over each of its nine edges).
3. Both uniform bounds are sharp. There is a two-orbit graph whose first dyadic length is 16, and a six-orbit graph whose first dyadic length is 32. With just one orbit the sharp bound is 4.

This is a statement for **unbounded graph order**. In particular it applies to every Cayley graph of valency at least three whose group has an abelian subgroup of index at most six: left multiplication by the subgroup is a semiregular action with that many orbits. No normality assumption on the subgroup is needed. It does not cover arbitrary cubic graphs.

## 1. Orbit templates

Fix one representative of each orbit and identify that orbit with A by translation. For i ≠ j, each edge orbit between layers i,j is a perfect matching, of the form (i,x) ∼ (j,x+a). For a single layer, an edge orbit is given by a nonzero displacement a and its negative. It contributes either one neighbor (2a=0, called a half-loop in the quotient) or two (a≠−a, an ordinary loop). In particular edge inversions have not been excluded.

Delete entire edge orbits as long as minimum degree at least three is preserved. The operation terminates, and any dyadic cycle found after deletion also lies in G. If the resulting quotient on layers is disconnected, each component supplies a template on at most r orbits and still has minimum degree at least three.

A connected edge-minimal template is specified by the number of half-loops s_i and pairs of opposite darts p_i at each vertex and the multiplicities m_ij between distinct vertices. Set d_i=s_i+2p_i+sum_j m_ij. The complete enumeration criteria are:

* d_i ≥ 3.
* s_i > 0 implies d_i=3; p_i > 0 implies d_i ≤ 4.
* m_ij > 0 implies min(d_i,d_j)=3.

Indeed, deleting a half-loop, ordinary loop, or matching changes exactly the degrees indicated by its contribution. These conditions are necessary and sufficient for deletion minimality. They imply s_i ≤ 3, p_i ≤ 2 and m_ij ≤ 3. All valid connected arrays are enumerated up to renaming the vertices; there are respectively **3, 6, 20, 81, 387, 2,104** for r=1,…,6. Some contain a proper set of layers inducing minimum degree ≥3, in which case the theorem for fewer layers applies without a voltage computation.

For r ≤ 5 the checker exhausts all off-diagonal arrays and allowed loop data. For six layers it first enumerates all connected simple off-diagonal supports up to isomorphism: build a connected graph by adding a vertex with a nonempty neighbor set to a smaller connected graph. This exhausts connected graphs because a leaf of a spanning tree may be deleted. It then colors the support edges by multiplicities 1,2,3, adds every admissible internal choice, and quotients by the automorphisms of that support. The search code used a separate enumeration of supports by adjacency bit masks and their permutation orbits.

## 2. How a finite certificate covers all abelian groups

For each template fix a spanning tree and change representatives one layer at a time to make its matching labels zero. Each other matching or ordinary loop has a formal label e_j; reversing its direction changes sign. Each half-loop has a label e_j with the additional relation 2e_j=0. With d formal labels, let L₀ ≤ Z^d be the subgroup generated by these relations. **Every concrete assignment factors through Z^d/L₀**, via the homomorphism taking the formal labels to their values in A. The labels do not need to generate A.

At a node of the certificate the current relation subgroup is L ⊇ L₀. Vertices of the formal graph are (i,x) for x in Z^d/L, with neighbors prescribed by adding the label of an outgoing dart. Consider any map of formal labels to A killing L. The following two rules prove a statement simultaneously for all such maps:

* **Invalid node.** If at some vertex a dart gives the vertex itself, or two distinct outgoing darts give the same neighbor, all its quotient assignments have the same degeneracy. None realizes the original simple, exact-degree template.
* **Cycle node.** Give a list of distinct formal vertices forming a simple cycle C of an allowed length. If the concrete images are distinct it is already the required cycle. Otherwise two vertices (i,x),(i,y) on the same layer coincide. Their nonzero difference is killed by the assignment, so it factors through the child relation subgroup L+Z(x−y). The certificate must prove the assertion at that child for *every* possible same-layer pair on C.

Strict enlargement prevents revisiting a node on a branch. A finite checked directed acyclic graph of these steps establishes the universal assertion by backward induction, not by testing finitely many sample groups or group orders. A cycle with no same-layer pair needs no children. The checker represents the row lattice L by a Hermite normal form. On adjoining a relation it recomputes the lattice basis by extended Euclidean row operations, which preserve the full integer row span (no rational saturation). It checks closure, distinctness, every adjacency and collision branch, and finally the claimed sharp bound template by template. No search-engine success flag is trusted.

The companion data and standard-library Python replay give a finite proof of the computational step. For r=6 there is **exactly one length-32 certificate node**, at the starting relation lattice for the loopless simple template with bipartition {0,1,2},{3,4,5} and all nine cross edges. All other six-orbit templates have certificates using only 4, 8 and 16, or reduce to fewer orbits. If a graph with six layers has no such shorter dyadic cycle, the deletion process therefore cannot split its quotient, and its remaining spanning subgraph is a lift of K₃,₃. This proves the structural assertion.

## 3. Sharpness

**Two layers.** On two copies of A=(Z/5Z)², join each x on the left to x, x+(1,0), x+(0,1) on the right. This gives a simple cubic graph with 50 vertices. Translation is transitive on either layer, and each cycle uses the left layer. An exact depth-first enumeration rooted at the left zero gives 0, 0 and 816 unoriented cycles of lengths 4, 8 and 16 through that vertex. See `sharpness_two.py`. The one-orbit case follows also by choosing two nonopposite connection steps a,b in a Cayley graph on an abelian group; 0,a,a+b,b make a square.

**Six layers.** Take three left and three right copies of Z/152Z and edge labels
```
0    0    0
0    1   84
0  141  121
```
from left to right, so that a matrix entry a joins x to x+a in its target layer. This 912-vertex graph is simple, cubic and connected: the zero labels form a spanning tree on layers, and the label 1 generates the fiber. The independent verifier `sharpness_six.py` enumerates all simple paths of length L/2 from one zero-coordinate representative in each left layer, and checks for a pair meeting only at their common endpoints. This is equivalent to a simple L-cycle through a representative. It finds no cycles of lengths 4, 8, or 16 and explicitly returns a simple 32-cycle. The finite witness therefore shows that the exceptional template is necessary. No assertion of smallest possible order is made.

## 4. A two-orbit extension without semiregularity

If an abelian automorphism group has at most two orbits, the bound of 16 holds even with arbitrary point stabilizers. The additional genuinely nonsemiregular cases have a 4- or 8-cycle.

For one orbit the action is regular modulo its kernel. For two orbits let H and K be their stabilizers in A; abelianness makes the stabilizer constant on each layer. If there are no cross edges, work on a transitive layer. If H=K, quotient out this common kernel and invoke the main theorem. If the subgroups are incomparable, choose h in H but not K and k in K but not H. Translating a cross edge uv (u stabilized by H, v by K) by 0,h,k,h+k gives a square on u, k+u, v, h+v.

It remains to assume H is a proper subgroup of K. Quotient out H, obtaining layers A' and A'/D with D=K/H nontrivial. The cross-neighborhood of a vertex x on the first layer depends only on x+D. If it has at least two vertices, x and x+h, for any nonzero h in D, give a 4-cycle through two common neighbors. Otherwise there is just one cross matching orbit, and after adjusting the representative in the second layer, x is joined there to x+D. The first layer has internal degree at least two. Two nonopposite steps give a 4-cycle by commutativity, so we may assume that its steps are exactly ±a with order(a)>2. If 2a ∈ D, the path 0,a,2a closes to a 4-cycle through the common hub D. If 2a ∉ D, the cosets D,a+D,2a+D are pairwise distinct. Take nonzero h in D. The paths 0,a,2a and h,h+a,h+2a on the first layer are vertex-disjoint. Their corresponding endpoints attach to the same two distinct hubs D and 2a+D, giving a simple 8-cycle.

## 5. Scope, sources and next obstruction

The original problem appears in Paul Erdős, [*Some old and new problems in various branches of combinatorics*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X%2896%2900173-2), Discrete Mathematics 165/166 (1997), item 3, p. 228. [Problem 64](https://www.erdosproblems.com/64) asks whether every finite simple graph of minimum degree three or greater has a cycle of length 2^k, k≥2. The affirmative statement is conventionally called a conjecture; the original authors in fact suspected a negative answer.

[Ducoffe and Dumitru](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28594) study minimal counterexamples and finite-order verification. [Burkhart](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05060) proves the conjecture for Cayley graphs of odd-order and nilpotent groups. The finite classification here instead bounds the number of orbits, does not bound graph order, and does not assume the graph is Cayley. Known honeycomb cycle-spectrum results overlap the special case of the two-vertex template with three matching edges; see [Alspach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.05133). The general concept of abelian-inevitable cycles appears in [Kelley and Walker](https://math.unl.edu/faculty/Kelley/Kelley_Walker_ISIT08.pdf). Importantly, [Cinna Davis's verification record](https://itsdavis.com/solved-conjectures/docs/lean-status/) already uses collision-branch certificates for abelian covers of one singly subdivided tetrahedron (which has a degree-two vertex). The collision principle itself is not offered as novel. A search for a prior sharp classification across *all* semiregular abelian orbit templates through six orbits did not locate one. That is not a proof of bibliographic novelty.

No argument here handles unbounded orbit counts. In fact the trivial group acts semiregularly on every graph, with one orbit per vertex, so a proof for all orbit counts would imply the original conjecture. A possible structural next step is to identify which templates force a short simple dyadic cycle before any quotient relations are imposed, and which break that cycle under quotienting; the K₃,₃ transition makes that distinction measurable. Merely increasing the finite orbit cutoff is not a substitute for that theorem.

## 6. A decision principle beyond the six-orbit computation

**Lemma.** Fix any connected template and any finite set of allowed cycle lengths. There is a terminating exact test of whether *all* its simple abelian realizations contain an allowed length. If not, the test can return a finite simple abelian realization avoiding every allowed length, even when the formal counterexample it first finds is infinite.

**Proof.** At each valid relation node, search all walks up to the largest allowed length in its formal graph from the zero-coordinate representative of each layer (translation handles all other starts). There are finitely many walks. If an allowed simple cycle occurs, branch on every nonzero same-layer collision as above. Each branch strictly enlarges a subgroup of the fixed finitely generated free abelian group Z^d. There is no infinite increasing chain: the union of such a chain is itself a subgroup of Z^d, hence finitely generated, and all its generators lie at a common finite stage. The tree is finitely branching, so an infinite tree would have an infinite branch. Thus the entire search terminates.

At a valid node without an allowed cycle, let Q=Z^d/L. Retain the nonzero displacements of all walks of length at most max(2, largest allowed length) starting at a zero-coordinate representative and ending in its own layer. This is a finite set. Residual finiteness of finitely generated abelian groups gives a finite quotient of Q killing none of these elements: in a decomposition into cyclic factors retain the finite factors and reduce the infinite coordinates modulo a number greater than the absolute values of all coordinates being retained. The finite derived graph has the same port distinctness (a collapsed loop or two coincident darts would give a newly closed walk of length one or two). Any new simple allowed cycle would lift to a walk in Q starting and ending in the same layer. By the choice of finite quotient its displacement was zero already, and the lift of a simple cycle has distinct intermediate vertices. This contradicts the absence of a formal cycle. This proves the lemma.

The lemma is not a decision procedure for the full Erdős question: both quotient size in layers and the upper bound on candidate lengths are fixed beforehand. The six-orbit theorem is the evaluated sharp case of this method, not a consequence of termination alone.

## Reproduction

From the unpacked directory, using regular Python 3 (not `python -O`):
```
python3 check.py
python3 sharpness_two.py
python3 sharpness_six.py
```
The checker and sharpness programs have no external dependencies. `certificates.json.gz` contains every literal relation-lattice certificate and orbit reduction; `CHECKED.txt` gives the expected replay summary. Optional search scripts `fast_covers.py` and `enumerate_large.py` are included; the former uses SymPy for independent lattice arithmetic during discovery. They are not required to verify the proof. No third-party code, proof assistants, live services or random trials are required by the checker.
