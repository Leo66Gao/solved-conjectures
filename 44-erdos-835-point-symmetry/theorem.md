# No point-transitive solution at the first open parameter of Erdős–Rosenfeld

**Theorem.** Suppose the 16-subsets of a 32-set have a proper 17-coloring for adjacency by a one-point exchange. The group G of point permutations preserving the color classes as a partition has no subgroup of order 16. In particular it is intransitive on the 32 points. This permits arbitrary relabeling of the colors by G. The theorem does **not** rule out an asymmetric coloring or resolve Erdős problem #835.

**1. Design counts and complements.** Every 15-set has a rainbow star of 17 one-point extensions. More generally the number of blocks of any given color containing a prescribed s-set (s<16) is λ_s=binom(32-s,16-s)/17: count incident 15-sets through it. In the general balanced problem on 2k points replace 16 by k and 17 by k+1. For any 16-set B and color class D, inclusion-exclusion for D-blocks disjoint from B gives 1_D(B^c)=K+1_D(B), because all counts at proper subsets are uniform and 16 is even. Summing over B gives K=0. Hence B and its complement always have the same color.

**2. Fixed elements of involutions.** Let g be a nonidentity point involution and F its number of fixed colors. On an invariant 15-set S, assigning to each outside point the color of the induced 16-set gives an equivariant bijection to the palette. Thus F is the number of fixed ground points outside S. If g has f fixed points and m=(32-f)/2 pairs, the attainable fixed-point counts i inside S are the odd values between max(0,15-2m) and min(f,15). For even 4≤f≤16 this includes 1 and 3; for 18≤f≤30 it includes 13 and 15. Each would give conflicting values of F, so f=0 or 2. For f=2 it gives F=1.

For f=0, a transversal of the 16 pairs is mapped to its complement, so uses a fixed color. Two neighboring transversals show F≥2. Take seven pairs as a 14-set S. There are λ_14=9 extensions in each color, so each fixed color must appear on at least one invariant extension. There are only nine in total. Equality F=9 would color the unions of eight pairs with nine colors, properly on J(16,8), since this argument applies to every seven pairs. But λ_5=binom(11,3)/9 is then nonintegral. Using parity of the palette, F∈{3,5,7}.

An involution g which is the square of an order-four permutation cannot have two fixed points: its moved-point count is a multiple of four, whereas 30 is not. Thus every order-four element fixes no point. Moreover an order-eight permutation would have fourth power moving 32 points and 10,12, or 14 colors; the latter number must be a multiple of eight, impossible. This excludes all higher 2-power orders.

**3. Fixed colors of order-four subgroups.** Every subgroup H of order four fixes at most three colors. If a nonidentity element of H fixes any point, it is an involution fixing only one color. Otherwise H acts freely on the points, with eight orbits of four. For three orbits S, each H-fixed color has λ_12=285 extensions, an odd number, hence at least one fixed extension. The invariant 16-set extensions add one of five unused orbits. Five fixed colors would therefore properly color J(8,4) with five colors by restriction to unions of four orbits. This is impossible: fixing one of the eight points would partition the triples of the other seven into five Steiner triple systems, whereas at most two such systems are disjoint. The latter classical finite fact is also checked by the 14-line `fano.py`: generate all systems by covering the first unused pair, then test all disjoint triples. There are 30 systems, 120 disjoint pairs, and no disjoint triple. Finally, the fixed-color count of H is odd, so it is at most three.

In any 2-subgroup P, each point stabilizer is elementary abelian because no element of order four fixes a point. It cannot contain a four-group: each of its three involutions would then fix exactly two points and Burnside would give (32+3·2)/4 point orbits, not an integer. Each point stabilizer thus has order at most two.

**4. Excluding order 16.** Suppose |P|=16. Any central involution z of P is fixed-point-free on points, since its two fixed points otherwise form a P-invariant pair, with point stabilizer of order at least eight. It consequently fixes three, five, or seven colors. The color action of P is faithful: any nontrivial kernel would contain an involution, whereas every involution fixes at most seven colors.

If P is abelian, on a color orbit of size four the order-four stabilizer would fix all four, impossible by §3. On an orbit of size eight an involution would fix all eight. A size-16 orbit would leave only one color fixed by each involution, impossible since all involutions are central. Thus every orbit has size one or two, with at most eight two-point orbits. On each pair precisely eight group elements interchange the colors. Total interchanges over all elements are at most 64. Faithfulness forces P to be elementary abelian (the action embeds in a product of order-two groups), and each of its 15 nonidentity elements moves at least ten colors, interchanging at least five pairs. But 15·5>64.

Now suppose P is nonabelian. Its center Z has size four. A larger center gives a cyclic quotient and forces commutativity. If |Z|=2, the quotient Q=P/Z has size eight. If abelian, commutators are central of order at most two, so every square is central and Q is elementary abelian. The commutator pairing Q×Q→Z would be a nondegenerate alternating form on a three-dimensional binary space, impossible. If Q is nonabelian, it has a normal cyclic subgroup of order four (take an element of order four). For a lift a of its generator, every conjugate of a equals a or its inverse modulo Z. Squaring shows a² is central since a⁴=1, contradicting that aZ has order four.

Thus P/Z is the four-group (a cyclic quotient again gives commutativity). For lifts a,b of its generators the derived group is generated by the nontrivial central involution d=[a,b]: all commutators are central, a²∈Z, and [a,b]²=[a²,b]=1. In a four-color orbit the induced group is nonabelian; otherwise the stabilizer would fix all four colors, contrary to §3. So d acts nontrivially on every such orbit, moving the entire orbit by centrality. On each orbit of size at least eight d cannot fix the whole orbit, so again moves it all. On size-one and size-two orbits d acts trivially. Its number of moved colors is a multiple of four; among 10,12,14 this must be 12. Exactly five colors are therefore in orbits of size one or two. The induced action on those five has order at most four, leaving a kernel of order at least four that fixes all five. This contradicts §3.

No P of order 16 exists. As every finite 2-group of order at least 16 has such a subgroup, the 2-part of |G| is at most eight. Orbit-stabilizer now excludes a transitive action on 32 points. □
