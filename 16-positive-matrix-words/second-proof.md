# Independent separation argument: maximal cyclic runs in an exponential series
Leo Gao — AI-assisted research. This supplement independently verifies the key nonreal-trace implication for arbitrary real powers; it does not use Gram matrices or the Hamilton-tour coefficient in the manuscript.

Fix a cyclically reduced word with L ≥ 2 syllables (c_j,p_j), with adjacent colors distinct cyclically and p_j nonzero. Write it as a formal exponential series F=∏ exp(p_j X_{c_j}) in noncommuting self-adjoint variables. Let F^rev be the same word in reversed order. If every Hermitian positive definite input gives real trace, then for all finite-dimensional Hermitian matrices X_c and real t we have Tr F(tX)=Tr F^rev(tX): products of exp(tp_j X_c) and their reversals are adjoints. Equating Taylor coefficients, each homogeneous noncommutative polynomial in F−F^rev has identically zero trace on all Hermitian tuples. The same is true on arbitrary complex tuples: the Hermitian matrices are a real form of the full complex matrix space, and a polynomial in the coordinates of a real basis vanishing for all real coordinate vectors vanishes for their complexifications.

For a polynomial homogeneous in all letters together of degree d, identically zero trace forces its sum of coefficients on each cyclic rotation class of ordinary words to be zero. To check this explicitly, take a directed cycle of length d and mark successive edges by the letters of the chosen ordinary word. For each color take the ordinary (not Hermitian) forward adjacency matrix consisting of its edges. The trace of a degree-d word counts its matching starting points, giving zero off the chosen rotation class and a common positive rotation-stabilizer factor on it. The cases d=1,2 also follow by a directed loop/digraph.

For positive integers n_1,...,n_L consider the ordinary word consisting of n_j copies in succession of each color c_j. Its cyclic sequence of monochromatic runs has length L. Any term producing its rotation class in F or F^rev must use a positive Taylor degree in every exponential: choosing degree zero in even one leaves at most L−1 cyclic runs. For a rotation to be produced by positive choices it must start at a run boundary, since degrees at both ends have distinct colors.

Fix the list c=(c_1,...,c_L) as the oriented indexing pattern. Form a finite multiset M of exponent tuples read by aligning a rotation of the original syllable cycle to this color pattern. Form N in the same way for the reversed syllable cycle. Equating cyclic coefficient sums yields, after multiplying by ∏ n_j!,
   
    Σ_{u∈M} ∏ u_j^{n_j}  =  Σ_{v∈N} ∏ v_j^{n_j}    for every n_j≥1.

In obtaining this equality we summed over all matching rotations with multiplicity; this merely multiplies each distinct contributing ordinary word by the rotation stabilizer of the test run necklace (c_j,n_j), the same factor on both sides. Both multisets contain only tuples with every coordinate nonzero.

These moments distinguish finite multisets off the coordinate hyperplanes: given any tuple u in their combined support, interpolate a polynomial as a product of coordinate Lagrange polynomials isolating u among the finite coordinate value sets. Multiply it by ∏ x_j to ensure no monomial has a zero coordinate degree; its value at u is still nonzero. Equality of the preceding sums forces equal multiplicities at u. Thus M=N. Since M contains the original exponent tuple, the reverse had to match a rotation preserving both colors and exponents. This contradicts nonreversibility.

So for a nonreversible cycle some positive definite tuple has nonreal trace. The tensor-power/realification step of the paper now gives negative real trace and full trace ranges. The Gram construction remains preferable: unlike this proof, it gives the complex nonreal witness in an order bound L independent of real exponent values.
