# Strictness of the path-variation bound
**Leo Gao · AI-assisted supplemental research note.**

**Theorem.** For every $0<a<1$, every packed convex $K$ in $x+y+az=t$ has $\tau^*(K)>1-a$. On each fixed compact interval of coefficients $J\subset(0,1)$ this improvement over $1-a$ is uniform over all feasible offsets and all packed $K$. In particular there is some nonexplicit $\epsilon>0$ for which the sharp conjectured bound $2/3$ holds at **every** feasible offset for $0\le a\le 1/3+\epsilon$.

Use the previous note's notation for a chosen horizontal coordinate $s$, the other reflected coordinate $W=1-t+s+az$, and the contact hull with vertices $L=(0,C), R=(1,D), P=(A,0), Q=(B,1)$ in $(s,z)$. Choose $s$ so that $A>B$, which is possible because the sum of the two candidate differences is $a>0$. Put $c=(t-1)/a$. For interior offsets $1<t<1+a$, we have $0<c<1$ and $C\ge c\ge D$.

## 1. Interior offsets: a strictly better path mixture, or an augmentable antidiagonal

If $0<D<C<1$ and $0<B<A<1$, the four density-difference vectors in the main proof have the origin in the interior of their hull (every nonzero functional takes both signs). They therefore admit a solution assigning positive weights to all four paths: first allocate a sufficiently small common positive weight to each, then represent the small residual displacement using their convex hull. Paths other than the vertical route have $V_-<1$. Hence the average variation is less than one and clipping the shear marginal loses strictly less than $a$.

If still $0<D<C<1$ but $A=1,B>0$, the lower and the vertical path vectors are respectively $(0,-1/C)$ and $(0,B/(1-C))$; their positive mixture gives the zero vector, and the lower path has $V_-=C<1$. If $B=0,A<1$, instead use the upper and the vertical paths, whose first components are opposite and whose second components vanish; $V_-$ for the upper path is $1-D<1$. In such cases a vertical segment with zero $s$ width at a path endpoint is simply omitted when parameterizing by $s$. If $A=1$ and $B=0$, the segment from $Q$ to $P$ is a full antidiagonal.

If $C=D=c\in(0,1)$, the lower path carries a $z$ marginal uniform on $[0,c]$, the upper path one on $[c,1]$. Mix with weights $c,1-c$ to get a uniform $z$ marginal and average downward variation $c^2+(1-c)^2<1$, including when one of these paths skips a zero-width endpoint segment.

If $C=1$ and $0<D<1$, the lower path and the straight diagonal $LR$ suffice when $A<1$. The lower path density on $(0,D)$ is $A+(1-A)/D>1$, and both paths have constant density on each of $(0,D),(D,1)$. Assign the lower path the reciprocal of that density and the straight path the remaining positive mass. The density is then one on both intervals by normalization, and the straight path has $V_-=1-D<1$. If $A=1$ the segment $LP$ is instead a full antidiagonal. In the case $0<C<1,D=0$, use the upper path and $LR$ when $B>0$: the upper path has density $(1-B)+B/(1-C)>1$ on $(C,1)$, so it receives the reciprocal of that density, and the rest of the mass uses $LR$ with $V_-=C<1$. For $B=0$ the segment $QR$ is a full antidiagonal. The last possibility $C=1,D=0$ gives a full antidiagonal directly via $LR$.

Whenever there is a full antidiagonal in $(s,z)$, its image under $W$ is an interval of length $1-a$. Put mass $1-a$ uniformly on the antidiagonal. Its $s,z$ marginals have density $1-a<1$ and its $W$ marginal has density one on that interval only. Since $K$ is packed in the remaining coordinate, it contains a point whose $W$ lies strictly outside the interval (at least one of 0 and 1 does). Connect it to an antidiagonal point that differs from it in all three projected coordinates. This is possible: the antidiagonal is injective in $s,z$, so at most two of its points are excluded, and every point on it differs in $W$. A short initial subsegment lies entirely outside the saturated $W$ interval and has nonzero length in all projections. Give it a sufficiently small positive uniform mass. It fits under the $s,z$ density slack $a$ and the $W$ density cap one, proving the strict improvement.

## 2. Endpoint offsets

At $t=1$, the two cube vertices $(1,0,0)$ and $(0,1,0)$ are forced into $K$ by the corresponding facet contacts. Choose any $z=1$ contact. Its $s$ coordinate $B$ is in $[0,1-a]$, and the projection contains the triangle $(0,0),(1,0),(B,1)$ in $(s,z)$. Here $W=s+az$. Consider the route from $(0,0)$ to $(B,1)$ to $(1,0)$ with uniform $s$ parameter. If $B=0$ omit the first edge of zero $s$ width. The $z$ marginal is uniform, since the two edges contribute respective constant densities $B$ and $1-B$ on $(0,1)$.

The first edge projects to $W\in[0,B+a]$ with density $B/(B+a)$. The second projects to $[B+a,1]$; retain the fraction $(1-B-a)/(1-B)$ of its mass, making its $W$ density one. If $B+a=1$ its $W$ projection is constant and the retained fraction is zero. The resulting measure has mass $1-a$. Its $z$ marginal has density $1-a$ throughout $(0,1)$; its $s$ marginal has positive slack on $(B,1)$; and its $W$ marginal has positive slack on $(0,B+a)$.

Inside this triangle there is an open region with $s>B$ and $W<B+a$. For example, start at any baseline $s\in(B,B+a)$ and raise $z$ a sufficiently small positive amount. Choose a short segment in this region varying all three coordinates, and give it a sufficiently small positive uniform mass, respecting the three slacks. This yields a strict improvement at $t=1$. Reflect all coordinates for $t=1+a$.

## 3. Uniformity on a compact coefficient range

Suppose there were no uniform improvement on a compact $J\subset(0,1)$. For a sequence violating such an improvement, choose six contacts and pass to a subsequence on which their coefficients, offsets, and all contact points converge. The equal-normal pair of first two coefficients is retained; call the limiting third coefficient $a_*>0$. The limiting contact hull has interior in its plane. Indeed, a packed segment would have absolute coordinate increments all equal to one, which cannot solve $\pm1\pm1\pm a_*=0$ for $a_*\in(0,1)$. The strict result gives a dominated measure of mass $m>1-a_*$ in the limiting hull.

Contract slightly toward an interior point and scale its mass by the contraction factor, preserving all marginal caps while keeping mass above $1-a_*$. Convolve in the plane with a smooth compactly supported nonnegative probability kernel of sufficiently small support. This preserves every cap by convolution and puts a smooth density compactly inside the limiting hull. Use $(s,z)$ coordinates, with the same choice of $s$ for all hulls in this argument. The nearby contact hull projections converge in Hausdorff distance, so they eventually contain this compact support (use the positive support-function margin). Its $s,z$ densities remain unchanged; the third density for the linear form $t-s-az$ varies uniformly with $a,t$ by the integral formula for projecting a smooth compactly supported density. Divide by $1+o(1)$ to restore all three caps, obtaining a uniform mass excess above $1-a_n$ for large $n$, a contradiction. Apply this around $a_*=1/3$ for the final consequence.
