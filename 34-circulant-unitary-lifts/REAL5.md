# All orthostochastic examples at order five lift to circulant orthogonals

**Theorem (with a small exact finite certificate).** If a real orthogonal `5×5` matrix has a circulant matrix of squared entries, a real circulant orthogonal matrix has those same squared entries. Thus any counterexample to the complex-lifting assertion at order five would have to be positive *and* have no real orthogonal lift. This does not decide whether such a complex example exists.

Write the displacement magnitudes as `a_0,...,a_4`. For each shift `h=1,2` call a sign vector balanced if `Σ_s e_s a_s a_{s+h}=0` (indices mod 5). Count vectors projectively, identifying simultaneous negation. Every pair of rows at distance h of an arbitrary real orthogonal lift supplies one such vector. Let `q_s` be the product of the signs down displacement diagonal s of the given lift. The coordinatewise product of the five row-pair sign vectors at distance h is precisely the vector `(q_s q_{s+h})_s`, since translation permutes rows.

If a shift has at most two projective balanced vectors, the product of any odd number of its balanced vectors remains balanced. Indeed among two types one occurs an odd number of times and the other an even number, and signs square to 1. If this holds for both h, the coefficients `q_s a_s` define the desired circulant orthogonal lift. The same argument works if there are zeros and the number of common nonzero positions per shift is at most three: there is at most one projective balancing on those nonzero positions (a singleton cannot cancel; two terms must oppose; among three positive lengths the largest must equal the other two combined). The empty overlap imposes nothing. On five points this covers every support with a zero, since any two different translates of a proper nonempty subset have intersection of size at most three. Set arbitrary signs on the unused entries.

It remains to consider strictly positive magnitudes where at least one shift has three or more projective balancings. By relabeling the cyclic group by multiplication by a unit take it to be `h=1`. Write `w_s=a_s a_{s+1}`. A partition into two sides of equal sum either has a singleton side (then it is unique), or has a distinguished side containing two of the five terms. All these pairs sum to the same number, half the total. Any two must intersect, because a disjoint pair of pairs would use all the total with a positive fifth term remaining. Three or more pairwise intersecting two-element subsets either have a common index or are the three edges of a triangle.

* If they have a common index of weight `u`, at least three other weights equal some `v>0`. The fifth weight is `u−v>0` by balance. Scaling by `v`, the five weights are `(1,1,1,t,1+t)` in some order, `t>0`.
* In the triangle case its three vertex weights are equal and the two remaining weights sum to one of them. After scaling the five weights are `(1,1,1,t,1−t)` in some order, `0<t<1`.

Translation and reflection of edge labels (induced by the corresponding index rotations/reflections of the original cyclic group) put the exceptional weights at 0 and `j∈{1,2}` in either case. Set `w_0=t, w_j=1+f t`, the rest 1, with `f∈{1,−1}`. The other-shift weights, in the same common normalization, are
```
v_i=a_i a_{i+2}=w_{i+2}w_{i+4}/w_{i+3}.
```
For each of the four arrangements the shift-2 balancing requirement restricts `t` to finitely many algebraic numbers. To certify the final finite enumeration, run `python3 verify_ortho5.py` (SymPy). It performs these exact steps:

1. Form all 32 signed sums of the five rational functions v, cancel denominators, and check that none of their numerators is identically zero. Factor over the rationals. Keep the irreducible factors with a real root in the admissible interval, excluding the denominator/zero-weight endpoints. The counts with `(f,j)=(+,1),(+,2),(−,1),(−,2)` are respectively 5,9,7,8. Distinct roots of a single irreducible factor need not be separated because all subsequent predicates are rational-polynomial equalities.
2. For each retained factor g, compute all balanced sign vectors at the two shifts by testing divisibility of the corresponding numerator by g. This is equivalent to vanishing at every root of g, since denominators are nonzero on the interval.
3. To test a general real lift, fix its first row and column signs to + by column then row sign changes (no zero entries). Generate the 16 possible subsequent rows of signs with positive first entry, recursively accepting a row if its coordinatewise product with each already selected row is balanced for the corresponding cyclic displacement. Reversing a pair handles displacements 3 and 4. Reaching row five is equivalent to finding an orthogonal lift: all norm constraints follow from normalization, and orthogonality of every pair has been checked.
4. Test all 16 possible circulant signings up to overall sign. The arbitrary-lift and circulant-lift booleans agree on every retained factor, in fact both are true for precisely two factors in each arrangement. See `CHECKS_ORTHO5.txt`. No approximate root estimates or numerical orthogonality tests are involved.

The finite search is a certificate for the exceptional algebraic parameter values only. The sign-product and intersecting-pairs arguments exhaust every other configuration.
