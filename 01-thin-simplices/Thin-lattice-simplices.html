Width and free joins of thin lattice simplices

Project Prometheus · Research note

26 November 2026

Status. The arguments below prove Conjectures 1.5 and 1.6 and answer Question 1.4 in Vadym Kurylenko, Thin simplices via modular arithmetic, also appearing as such in the 2026 journal article. The targeted literature search found no earlier proof of these geometric statements; that is not a priority guarantee. The character method and group-covering bounds below have established precedents; the deductions here concern lattice simplices.

Theorem. Let Δ\Delta be a full-dimensional thin lattice simplex of dimension d≥1d\ge 1. 1. If dd is odd, an affine integral function takes the values 0 and 1 on the vertices of Δ\Delta, with an odd number of vertices in each fiber. In particular, Δ\Delta has lattice width one. 2. If Δ\Delta has lattice width at least two, its normalized volume is at most (d+1)!(d+1)!. In particular, there are only finitely many such simplices up to affine unimodular equivalence in every fixed dimension. 3. In each fixed dimension, only finitely many spanning thin simplices are not free joins. None exist in odd dimensions.

Barycentric coordinates modulo 1

Choose coordinates in which the ambient lattice is ℤd\mathbb Z^d. Write the vertices as v0,…,vdv_0,\dots,v_d, put n=d+1n=d+1, and let AA be the invertible n×nn\times n matrix with columns (vi,1)(v_i,1). The finite group C=A−1ℤn/ℤn⊂(ℝ/ℤ)n C=A^{-1}\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z^n\subset (\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^n has order |detA|=Volℤ(Δ)|\det A|=\operatorname{Vol}_{\mathbb Z}(\Delta), because multiplication by AA identifies it with ℤn/Aℤn\mathbb Z^n/A\mathbb Z^n. For each c∈Cc\in C, the representative in [0,1)n[0,1)^n gives exactly one lattice point of the half-open fundamental parallelepiped, by applying AA. The open parallelepiped consists of representatives with every coordinate nonzero. Since the local h*h^*-polynomial counts its lattice points by height, thinness is equivalent to saying each c∈Cc\in C has at least one zero coordinate.

For i∈I={0,…,d}i\in I=\{0,\dots,d\}, put χi(c)=e2πici\chi_i(c)=e^{2\pi i c_i} and Hi=kerχiH_i=\ker\chi_i. For S⊆IS\subseteq I, put χS=∏i∈Sχi\chi_S=\prod_{i\in S}\chi_i. In particular, χI=1\chi_I=1, since the last row of AA is all ones. An affine integral function with values 1 precisely on the vertices indexed by SS exists if and only if χS=1\chi_S=1. Indeed, the unique real affine function giving those values is x↦𝟏S𝖳A−1(x,1), x\longmapsto \mathbf{1}_S^\mathsf T A^{-1}(x,1), where 𝟏S\mathbf{1}_S is the vector of indicators. If χS=1\chi_S=1, the row 𝟏S𝖳A−1\mathbf{1}_S^\mathsf T A^{-1} is integral, since its value on each integer z∈ℤnz\in\mathbb Z^n is integral by applying the character condition to A−1z+ℤnA^{-1}z+\mathbb Z^n. Conversely, an affine integral function with the stated vertex values is given by an integral row of this form, which implies χS=1\chi_S=1. Thus width one is equivalent to the existence of a nonempty proper such SS: the resulting integral affine function is nonconstant and has width one. Conversely, an integral linear form attaining width one can be shifted to give integer vertex values in {0,1}\{0,1\}.

The character identity and odd dimension

For any J⊆IJ\subseteq I such that ⋃i∈JHi=C\bigcup_{i\in J}H_i=C, one factor of ∏i∈J(1−χi(c))\prod_{i\in J}(1-\chi_i(c)) vanishes at every c∈Cc\in C. Expanding and averaging yields 0=1|C|∑c∈C∏i∈J(1−χi(c))=∑S⊆JχS=1(−1)|S|.(1) 0=\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{c\in C}\prod_{i\in J}(1-\chi_i(c)) =\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq J\\ \chi_S=1}}(-1)^{|S|}. \quad\text{(1)} Here the average of a character is 1 if trivial and 0 otherwise: translating the summation by an element where the character is not 1 multiplies the sum by a scalar different from 1 without changing it.

For a thin simplex J=IJ=I is a cover. When dd is odd, nn is even, so S=⌀S=\varnothing and S=IS=I contribute +1+1 each in (1). There must therefore be an odd-cardinality SS with χS=1\chi_S=1. It is nonempty and proper, and its complement is odd as well. The affine integral function above proves the first assertion.

Irredundant covers and bounded volume

Suppose the width is at least two. If a proper J⊊IJ\subsetneq I covered CC, the empty subset would contribute +1+1 to (1), so there would be a nonempty S⊆JS\subseteq J with χS=1\chi_S=1. This would contradict width at least two. Hence the coordinate-kernel cover is irredundant: no proper subcollection covers. Moreover ⋂i∈IHi={0}\bigcap_{i\in I}H_i=\{0\}, because the coordinate characters jointly distinguish every element from zero.

Here is an elementary finite-group bound, the subgroup case of Tomkinson’s factorial bound for irredundant covers by cosets. Suppose a finite group GG has an irredundant cover by nn subgroups. Select the subgroups in a suitable order. If j<nj<n have already been selected, let KK be their intersection, with K=GK=G for j=0j=0. By irredundancy there is xx outside their union, and the coset xKxK is disjoint from each selected subgroup: xk∈Hxk\in H with k∈K⊆Hk\in K\subseteq H would imply x∈Hx\in H. The remaining n−jn-j subgroups cover xKxK, so some remaining HH meets it in at least |K|/(n−j)|K|/(n-j) elements. If y∈H∩xKy\in H\cap xK, then H∩xK=y(H∩K)H\cap xK=y(H\cap K); thus [K:K∩H]≤n−j[K:K\cap H]\le n-j. Select HH next. Multiplying the index bounds along the chain proves that the full intersection has index at most n!n!.

Applying this bound to CC yields Volℤ(Δ)=|C|≤n!\operatorname{Vol}_{\mathbb Z}(\Delta)=|C|\le n!. To see finiteness, put B=n!B=n!. Every coordinate of every group CC of order at most BB is killed by lcm(1,…,B)\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,B), so such coordinate groups are subgroups of a single finite group. The ordered coordinate group determines the simplex up to affine unimodular equivalence: if A′A' is another lifted vertex matrix with the same CC, then equality of the full preimages A−1ℤn=A′−1ℤnA^{-1}\mathbb Z^n=A'^{-1}\mathbb Z^n implies A′A−1∈GLn(ℤ)A'A^{-1}\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z). Its last row is (0,…,0,1)(0,\ldots,0,1), since it sends each lifted vertex to the corresponding lifted vertex and both have last coordinate 1. Thus its action at height one is affine unimodular. This proves the second assertion.

Spanning simplices and free joins

A lattice simplex is spanning if the differences from one lattice point of the simplex to all its lattice points generate the ambient lattice. A free join of two lattice polytopes P⊂ℝaP\subset\mathbb{R}^a, Q⊂ℝbQ\subset\mathbb{R}^b is conv((P,0,0)∪(0,Q,1))⊂ℝa+b+1 \operatorname{conv}\big((P,0,0)\cup(0,Q,1)\big)\subset\mathbb{R}^{a+b+1} in the direct-sum product lattice, up to affine unimodular equivalence; point factors are allowed.

Every spanning lattice simplex of width one is a free join. To see this, choose an affine integral form realizing width one and normalized to take vertex values 0 and 1. Its two extremal faces F0,F1F_0,F_1 contain all the lattice points of the simplex, because the form is integer-valued. Choose vertices pi∈Fip_i\in F_i, and let LiL_i be the subgroup generated by differences u−piu-p_i with u∈Fi∩ℤdu\in F_i\cap\mathbb Z^d. The vertices of a simplex are affinely independent, so the two face directions and the line ℝ(p1−p0)\mathbb R(p_1-p_0) form a direct sum of ℝd\mathbb R^d: bases are obtained from the edge differences within each vertex block and the one difference between the chosen vertices. Since the simplex is spanning, the same generators from all lattice points give ℤd=L0⊕L1⊕ℤ(p1−p0). \mathbb Z^d=L_0\oplus L_1\oplus\mathbb Z(p_1-p_0). Translate by −p0-p_0 and use bases of the two groups LiL_i, together with p1−p0p_1-p_0, as coordinates. The faces then occupy exactly the free-join normal form, proving the claim.

Consequently a spanning thin simplex that is not a free join has width at least two. The volume bound proves finiteness in each fixed dimension, and the odd-dimensional theorem rules out such examples in odd dimensions. These are the two answers to Question 1.4.

Related work

Kurylenko’s §2 supplies the modular barycentric model and its thinness and width criteria. Equation (1) is the identity coefficient of the vanishing group-ring product ∏i∈J([1]−[χi])\prod_{i\in J}([1]-[\chi_i]). The corresponding Fourier-product/cover method for finite-field hyperplanes appears, for instance, in Nagy, Pach and Tomon, Additive bases, coset covers, and non-vanishing linear maps, Lemma 4.2. Their Theorem 1.2 also supplies the sharper asymptotic volume bound exp(O(dloglogd))\exp(O(d\log\log d)) by applying its abelian-group cover bound to the coordinate kernels. Neither of the main arguments above depends on that theorem.

Scope checks

  • The ambient lattice is the one used to define width; the first two assertions do not require spanning. Neither proof assumes prime exponent or a squarefree normalized volume.
  • The bound concerns simplices of width at least two. It does not assert a bound for width-one thin simplices, or settle analogous questions for arbitrary polytopes.
  • Parity is necessary in part 1: for even d≥2d\ge2, twice the unimodular dd-simplex is thin and has width 2. For this example the barycentric coordinates are 0 or 1/2 with an even number of half-coordinates, so the all-nonzero vector cannot occur when d+1d+1 is odd. Every nonzero integral form takes values on the vertices that are all even, and a coordinate form attains width 2.
  • We do not use the exponential bound claimed in arXiv:2205.03389; that preprint was withdrawn.
