# Exact c=6 and c=7 checks

This is a finite corollary of the signed-kernel theorem in NOTES.md, not a check of a cutoff on the original graph order. It covers every connected simple input graph with the indicated cyclomatic numbers, by proof of the normal form. Everything below uses integers.

## Reproduction

From this directory:

    python3 cubic_kernels.py 10 > k6-large.txt
    python3 cubic_kernels.py 12 > k7-large.txt
    g++ -O3 -std=c++17 -Wall -Wextra -Werror find_violation.cpp -o find_violation_checked
    gzip -cd k6-large.txt.gz | ./find_violation_checked
    gzip -cd k7-large.txt.gz | ./find_violation_checked

The C++ program is deliberately restricted to the dimension-slack-at-most-one cases; its successful outputs are recorded in exact-c6-c7.txt. The 128-bit intermediate products, differences, and negation for gcd use checked arithmetic; any overflow throws rather than producing a negative result. The unsigned state counter remains in range for these inputs. The generation/check scripts have no external package requirements beyond Python 3 and a C++17 compiler (GCC or Clang for overflow intrinsics).

## Why the census is exhaustive

For target c, a violating score needs
    signature(P restricted to ker B) − number of marks ≥ floor((3c−1)/2)+1 = b.
The branch kernel has at most 2c−2 vertices, so only orders 9 and 10 at c=6, and 11 and 12 at c=7, can be relevant. At maximum order every degree is three. The next smaller order has one degree-four vertex (the excess over cubic is one) and every other degree is three.

All connected cubic multigraphs of even order are generated by the following induction, counting loops twice in degrees. At order two, the possibilities are a triple edge, or one loop at each vertex joined by an edge.
* If there is a nonloop edge which is not a bridge, delete it and suppress its two now degree-two endpoints. Connectedness is preserved. Reversing that move inserts two vertices on the old edges (possibly both on one old edge) and puts a new link between the inserted vertices.
* Otherwise the nonloop edges form a tree. A leaf of this tree has one loop; delete the leaf and its loop, and suppress its now degree-two neighbor. Reversing that move subdivides an edge and attaches a new looped vertex by an edge.
For order greater than two, these suppressions retain the usual connected cubic multigraph, with loops and parallel edges allowed. Every graph with one degree-four vertex can be obtained by splitting its four incidences into two pairs, putting one pair at each of two new vertices, and adding one connecting edge. This connected cubic multigraph appears in the previous generation; contracting that connecting edge recovers the original kernel. The code uses color refinement followed by exhaustive individualization within a deterministically selected color class to deduplicate. The canonical key is an actual adjacency encoding; equal keys always imply isomorphism. For the small cubic orders, counts 2, 5, 17, 71, 388, 2592 independently agree with [Chan (2012), *Combinatorics of the tropical Torelli map*](https://msp.org/ant/2012/6-6/ant-v6-n6-p03-s.pdf) and [A005967](https://oeis.org/A005967).

## Why the state pruning is exhaustive

Write t=k−b, where t is either zero or one. A violation requires that the rank of B plus the number of marks be at most t. Replacing a negative direct loop by a positive direct loop only increases a diagonal. An internal-zero loop with canceling, opposite port signs has identically zero coupling, so its state also is dominated by the positive direct loop (the extra independent zero variable changes no signature). Thus any surviving loop state is either positive direct or a nontrivial constraint that kills its branch coordinate. Connectedness and the maximum degree four imply at most one loop at any vertex in the candidate kernels.

Changing the sign of a branch coordinate is a congruence and changes the sign product of all its nonloop edges. Internal vertices of a constrained edge can also be switched to normalize their first port. Hence we can take all nonloop signs on a fixed spanning tree positive and enumerate only the remaining signs.

No constraints, exactly one nontrivial constraint, or rank greater than one are the only possibilities. Rank one consists of a single loop constraint, or a nonempty set of parallel nonloop edges whose constraint signs agree. Different pairs have linearly independent supporting rows; opposing signs also give independent rows. A mark may be selected only when t=1 and there is no nontrivial constraint. These are exactly the states checked. Signed compression after a constraint either deletes a loop's variable or identifies two variables up to sign.

After pruning, any matrix relevant to violation needs signature at least its dimension minus one. Even one negative eigenvalue makes that impossible, since it costs at least two relative to dimension. Thus it suffices to test positive semidefiniteness and rank. Symmetric congruence with a positive diagonal pivot reduces this problem to the exact Schur complement, multiplying by a positive factor and dividing by a positive common gcd. A negative diagonal disproves semidefiniteness; if every remaining diagonal is zero, semidefiniteness is equivalent to the whole remaining matrix being zero. The number of positive pivots of a positive semidefinite matrix is its rank. This does not use floating-point eigenvalues or tolerance.

## Separate verification

The script check_lower_examples.py independently reconstructs the published alternating 5-cycle/4-cycle chains for c=1,…,7, linking adjacent bridge ports on every interior cycle. It computes the adjacency signature of each line graph directly using rational congruence, not the reduced formula. Results are in lower-example-checks.txt.

The script check_normal_form.py is a non-exhaustive regression exercising loops, parallel paths, long decorated paths and hanging trees. Each of its 125 deterministic random inputs and its simple-graph normal form are rated by exact rational congruence, with equality checked between expanded and compressed representations and a nondecrease assertion relative to the input. Results are in normal-form-regressions.txt. These tests supplement, not replace, the proof of the normal form.
