# A cubic transport-cost law for controlled edge averaging
Research note by **Leo Gao**, with AI assistance (26 Nov 2026). Discovered while trying to change the schedule underlying the distinct-subset-sum construction, not offered as a resolution of Erdős's sharp-order problem. Literature priority and significance have not been externally assessed.

On a connected undirected unweighted graph, place unit mass at a root o. In one move, choose an edge with endpoint masses a,b and choose λ in [0,1/2]. Replace the pair by ((1−λ)a+λb, λa+(1−λ)b); nothing else changes. Edge choices and sharing fractions may be adaptive. After T individual edge updates let p be the resulting probability measure and d(v)=dist(o,v).

**Theorem (uniform sharp exponent).** For every integer R≥0,
    
    sum_v p(v) (d(v)−R)_+ ≤ sqrt(T/(R+1)).
    
Consequently E_p[d] ≤ (3/2^(2/3)) T^(1/3)+1. For every fixed 0<η<1, achieving p{d≥r}≥η costs at least cη²r³ updates, and on every graph on which distances from o are unbounded it can be accomplished in at most C_η r³ updates, uniformly in the graph. More generally the supremum of E_p[d] over schedules on any rooted graph with root eccentricity D is of order min{D,T^(1/3)}, with absolute implicit constants for T≥1. Thus the exponent 3 is sharp, even if the graph and strategy may change. The order is for individual pairwise edge moves, not rounds of many simultaneous moves or moves that redistribute all mass at a vertex among *all* its neighbors.

**Proof of the upper bound on displacement.** For every intermediate profile, the known pointwise inequality p(v)≤1/(1+d(v)) holds, for precisely this dynamics ([Gantert and Vilkas, Theorem 2.1](https://alea.impa.br/articles/v22/22-32.pdf), extending Gollin et al.). For completeness the short invariant behind it is p(S)≤1−∏_{v∈S}b_v for finite S, where b_v=d(v)/(d(v)+1). It is true initially. A full half-averaging on edge uv changes p(S) only if exactly one endpoint, say u, is in S. The new mass is the mean of the old masses of S∪{v} and S\{u}; the invariant is preserved since 1+b_ub_v≥2b_u, equivalent to d(u)≤d(v)+1. A partial update is a convex combination of doing nothing and full averaging, so the invariant holds there too. Take S={v}.

Set h=1/(R+1) and use the nonnegative convex function F(x)=x² for 0≤x≤h, F(x)=2hx−h² for x≥h. The global energy sum_v F(p(v)) never increases under pairwise averaging, and initially equals 2h−h²≤2h. Consider a move whose endpoints have different values of g(v)=(d(v)−R)_+. Their distances differ by exactly one, and the smaller distance is at least R. Their masses are both at most h. Thus the squared energy decrease on this move equals 2λ(1−λ)(a−b)²≥2δ², where δ=λ|a−b| is the mass transferred. Summed over all such moves, ∑δ²≤h. The expectation of g starts at 0, and the absolute change on each counted move is at most δ, with zero change on all other moves. Cauchy–Schwarz gives E[g]≤sqrt(T h). Adding R and choosing R=ceil((T/4)^(1/3)) proves the displacement estimate. For r≥2 choose R=floor(r/2), giving T≥η²(r−R)²(R+1)≥η²r³/8. The r=1 claim follows with any smaller universal positive constant.

**Independent proof of sharpness (no subset-sum input).** Choose a geodesic with N vertices numbered 0 through N−1, starting at o. At each of T=(N−1)N² steps choose one of its N−1 edges independently and uniformly and perform a full average. A decreasing initial profile remains decreasing after each operation, so every schedule has its maximum at 0. In expectation the mass after T moves is the T-th power of H=I−L/[2(N−1)] applied to the initial atom, where L is the unweighted path Laplacian (degree minus adjacency). Indeed each edge update subtracts half the rank-one edge Laplacian, and independence iterates the mean matrix. The L eigenvectors for m=0,...,N−1 have entries cos((j+1/2)mπ/N), j=0,...,N−1, of respective squared norms N for m=0 and N/2 otherwise. Substitution in the interior difference recurrence and the reflected endpoint conditions verifies eigenvalues 2(1−cos(mπ/N)), which are distinct. Thus H has nonnegative eigenvalues for N≥2, and the expected endpoint mass is at most
     
     1/N + (2/N) sum_{m=1}^{N−1} exp[−T(1−cos(mπ/N))/(N−1)] < 2/N.
     
Here 1−cos(mπ/N)≥2m²/N² and sum_{m≥1} exp(−2m²)≤1/(exp(2)−1)<1/2. Consequently at least one deterministic schedule has endpoint mass (and hence every site mass) <2/N. Take N=ceil(3r/(1−η))+1. The first r sites carry at most 2r/N, and the other sites have graph distance ≥r by geodesicity. This gives a deterministic strategy with fewer than N³ edge updates for any 0<η<1 and r≥1. It also gives maximal mean displacement ≥c T^(1/3) for all large T (choose η=1/2, appropriate r, and idle the remaining steps).

For the uniform finite-eccentricity statement take N=min(D+1,floor(T^(1/3))) whenever N≥8; an N-vertex geodesic exists, the schedule fits, and at least half the mass is at distance ≥floor(N/4), so E[d]≥N/16. If this choice gives N<8 and D≥1, one average of the root with a neighbor gives mean distance 1/2, which suffices after decreasing the universal constant. If D=0 the assertion is trivial. The upper bound follows by combining the proved O(T^(1/3)) bound with E[d]≤D.

**Identification with the arithmetic walk.** On K+1 wires numbered 0,...,K, a sweep averages (0,1),(1,2),...,(K−1,K). Apply this sweep then reverse numbering. Starting on real wire i+1 (i≥1), the mass advances through consecutively chosen gates, each halving the probability to continue. The two rightmost pre-reversal wire values are equal after the last gate. After reversal, merge wires {0,1} into class 0; real j+1 is class j for j≥1. Initial real wires 0 and 1 have identical transitions since the first gate averages them. From class i the class-0 probability after the step is 2^{i+1−K}; the probability of class j is 2^{i+j−K} for 1≤j≤K−1−i, zero otherwise. This is P in the [reflected-layer construction, §3](https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs/blob/main/pdf/Erdos1b.pdf), verified separately on 60 exact rational cases. After 2s sweeps in alternating physical directions the reversal cancels, so real endpoint mass is half of (P^{2s})_{00}; the two members of class 0 have equal mass. In particular these uniform sweeps are also an explicit O(r³) (not only probabilistic existence) attaining schedule by that paper's Proposition 3.1.

**Consequences.** On a path starting from its endpoint the mass vector stays monotone. If all masses are at most ε, at least half the mass must have reached distance floor(1/(2ε)), hence T≥c ε^(−3). No adaptive adjacent-pair schedule can improve the exponent for this peak-flattening task; alternating sweeps attain it. In the reflected construction q/2^r is exactly that peak for a sweep circuit of 2sK edge updates with r=2s(K−1)+1 graph labels. This blocks replacing the sweep ordering by a different neighboring-wire schedule with comparable label cost **if** the targeted normalized determinant still equals the endpoint mass; it does not claim arbitrary schedules yield admissible subset-sum labels. For arbitrary probability starting measures μ, conditioning on the initial site and holding the chosen move sequence fixed gives a coupling with W₁(μ,μ_after) ≤ (3/2^(2/3)) T^(1/3)+1. This argument also applies to a sequence originally chosen adaptively, after its realization is fixed.

**Provenance and scope.** The SAD (Sharing a Drink) operation is established [Häggström–Hirscher](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/519008/file/519008_Fulltext.pdf); the pointwise cap is prior art. [Florescu–Peres–Rácz (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1125923) studied the closely related question for controlled **vertex toppling**, with dimension-dependent cost Θ(r^{d+2}) on Z^d, not for these pairwise moves. This is not an improvement to their theorem. In the edge-average model one can select a geodesic and ignore transverse edges, which is impossible for their vertex-toppling rule. No claim of a previously named open question solved here. A targeted search found sources for SAD, stochastic and deterministic gossip, and optimal vertex-toppling, but did not establish priority of the cubic bound for controlled pairwise averaging. The transport theorem and its sharpness proof above require no subset-sum input; the optional sweep identification relates it to the original number-theoretic application.

## Time to put a fixed fraction of the maximum possible mass at a designated vertex

This stronger consequence answers a finite-time version of the *existing* pointwise water-transport extremum, rather than just escape to a set. Let o,v be specified vertices in an arbitrary connected graph, D=dist(o,v)≥1, and start with a unit atom at o. The attainable supremum at v is 1/(D+1) by the known pointwise cap and arbitrarily accurate equilibration on a shortest path. **For each fixed 0<c<1, the minimum number of elementary edge updates needed to attain mass at least c/(D+1) is Θ_c(D³), uniformly in o,v and the graph.** Side branches and adaptive scheduling cannot improve the order. No claim is made for c=1.

To prove the lower bound, fix an arbitrary sequence of T allowed moves, with their symmetric matrices A_1,...,A_T. A sequence originally chosen adaptively may be fixed after realization. Let p_0=δ_o, p_s=A_s p_{s−1}; let q_T=δ_v and q_{s−1}=A_s q_s. Each q_s is obtained from δ_v by applying the remaining matrices in reverse order. Set a=floor(D/3), b=D−a, and define g(x) as (dist(o,x)−a)/(b−a) clipped to [0,1]. Thus g(o)=0 and g(v)=1. On any edge ij for which g(i)≠g(j), the distances of its two endpoints to o are integers in [a,b]; the distances to v are therefore at least D−b=a. Hence all the p- and q-values there are bounded by h=1/(1+a)≤3/D, and |g(i)−g(j)|≤1/(b−a)≤3/D.

Telescoping the scalar B_s=sum_x g(x)p_s(x)q_s(x) from 0 to T gives exactly p_T(v). If the s-th update on ij has mixing coefficient λ, symmetry of A_s gives
     
    B_s−B_{s−1} = λ [g(i)−g(j)] [p_{s−1}(j)q_s(i)−p_{s−1}(i)q_s(j)].

Write δ_s^p=λ|p_{s−1}(i)−p_{s−1}(j)| and δ_s^q=λ|q_s(i)−q_s(j)|. On a changing edge the last bracket times λ is at most h(δ_s^p+δ_s^q) in absolute value, by adding and subtracting p_{s−1}(i)q_s(i). Apply the clipped-quadratic energy separately to the forward and backward profiles: on all changing edges the squared transfers sum to ≤h in either direction. Cauchy–Schwarz yields the off-diagonal kernel bound
     
    p_T(v) ≤ 2 h^(3/2) sqrt(T)/(b−a) ≤ 18 sqrt(3) sqrt(T) / D^(5/2).
     
Together with p_T(v)≥c/(D+1), this forces T≥c²D⁵/[972(D+1)²]≥c²D³/3888. This assertion is particular to serial symmetric diffusive moves; no independent increments or assumptions on the degrees are used.

For the matching upper bound use exactly the geodesic from o to v, with N=D+1 vertices. Choose T=ceil(B(N−1)N²) independent uniform full-edge averages as in the path spectral calculation above. The expected mass at the far endpoint is at least N^(−1)[1−2 sum_{m≥1} e^(−2Bm²)], since each nonconstant eigenvector contributes a signed endpoint product with absolute value ≤2/N. Choose B large enough in terms of c alone that 2/(e^(2B)−1)≤1−c. The expected target mass is ≥c/N, so there is a deterministic schedule achieving it. This proves the two-sided estimate and gives the explicit upper cost O(D³ log(2/(1−c))). The lower estimate does not determine the sharp dependence on c.

Further literature distinction: [Gollin et al., *Sharing tea on a graph*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15353) even allow averaging entire connected vertex sets in one operation. If that permission is used as the cost unit, a single averaging of a shortest path transports exactly 1/(D+1) to the target, and our cubic conclusion is false. Here one move acts on *one edge only*. The distinction is necessary and shows why finite-strategy results for connected-set averaging cannot be invoked for the conclusion here. Computational check: verify_commutator.py independently evaluates both sides of each telescoping identity as Fractions on 128 seeded forward/backward path histories and checks the resulting off-diagonal estimate; verify_transport.py exhaustively checks the one-sided bounds on 288,305 graph-state/time pairs with mixing weights in {0,1/4,1/2}. These are secondary diagnostics; the proofs are analytic.
