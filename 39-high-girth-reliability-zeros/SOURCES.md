# Sources and priority check (26 November 2026)

* Jason I. Brown and Charles J. Colbourn, [“Roots of the reliability polynomial”](https://doi.org/10.1137/0405047), *SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics* 5 (1992), 571–585. Their variables may be translated between edge-survival and edge-failure probability. For the failure convention, Corollary 3.2 is the real-root exclusion and Proposition 5.1 is multigraph density; see also Buys's precisely formulated restatement.
* Jason I. Brown and Isaac McMullin, [“On the real reliability roots of graphs”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09059), [journal paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2026.03.037). The original conjecture concerns *all simple graphs*, not our extra restrictions.
* Mohamed Omar, [“Real reliability roots of simple graphs are dense”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03530). Obtains unrestricted real density using substitutions by complete graphs missing an edge.
* Pjotr Buys, [“Density of reliability roots of simple graphs in the unit disk”](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07541v1). Theorem 1.1 settles both density statements for unrestricted simple graphs using a cycle with clique edge substitutions. Section 4 asks different questions, regarding complete graphs and closures for all multigraphs versus simple graphs. **This manuscript does not claim to resolve either question.** The known clique construction has a two-vertex separator for the relevant large parameters and girth three, and is not regular. [Buys's Lean repository](https://github.com/Pjotr5/ReliabilityRoots) describes formal verification for the unrestricted real case, not the present strengthening.
* A. Lubotzky, R. Phillips and P. Sarnak, [“Ramanujan graphs”](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02126799), *Combinatorica* 8 (1988), 261–277; [full primary text](https://courses.grainger.illinois.edu/cs598sgt/sp2015/LPS.pdf). Theorems 3.4 and 4.1 provide exactly the girth, order and eigenvalue bounds used here. If the graph is nonbipartite, its bipartite double cover retains these properties.
* F. Bencs, C. Piombi and G. Regts, [“On the complex zeros and the computational complexity of approximating the reliability polynomial”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11504). Their dynamical constructions include results on zeros outside the unit circle and hardness for planar graphs; these are different from an interior density theorem under the simultaneous structural restrictions here.
* [The innermost reliability zeros of complete graphs and Waldvogel's constant](https://evidencepress.org/releases/complete-graph-reliability-zeros/), public research announcement dated 26 November 2026. Concerns root counts, inner zeros of complete graphs, and a weak distributional limit for matroids, not existence of roots near a prescribed point inside the disk in maximally connected high-girth bipartite graphs. Weak convergence of the normalized zero measures to the unit circle does *not* rule out a vanishing proportion of interior zeros.

**Novelty limits.** Searches used “reliability roots” with regular, vertex connectivity, high girth, dense, bipartite, cyclic covers, and searches under the Tutte/connected-spanning-subgraph terminology. The checked sources and discoverable AI-assisted claims did not identify the simultaneous regular, maximally vertex-connected, bipartite, unbounded-girth density strengthening. This is not an exhaustive bibliographic or expert priority determination. This work claims neither density for one fixed degree nor that arbitrary high-girth graphs usually exhibit such zeros.
