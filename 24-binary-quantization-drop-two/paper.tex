\begin{abstract}
For a uniform binary $n$-vector observed through independent binary symmetric
noise, transmitting $n-2$ coordinates is optimal among deterministic
quantizers with at most $2^{n-2}$ labels, at every crossover probability.
The proof permits unequal cell sizes. A dimension-independent reduction
leaves 214 compressed shapes of sizes at most 16, and exact rational
certificates verify the only finite entropy comparisons.
\end{abstract}

\section{Main statement}
Work in nats and write $h(p)=-p\ln p-(1-p)\ln(1-p)$ and
$C(p)=\ln 2-h(p)$. Let $X$ be uniform on $\{0,1\}^n$ and
$Y=X\oplus Z$, with independent Bernoulli$(p)$ noise independent of $X$.
\begin{theorem}
For $n\ge2$, $0\le p\le1/2$ and every deterministic $U=f(X)$ with at
most $2^{n-2}$ labels, $I(U;Y)\le(n-2)C(p)$. Reading $n-2$ coordinates
attains the bound.
\end{theorem}
For a nonempty cell $A$ of size $m$, let $J(A)$ be the information between a
uniform input on $A$ and its noisy output. We show
\[
 J(A)\ge\left(\frac72-\frac6m\right)C(p),
 \qquad J(A)\ge\frac72 C(p)\quad(m\ge16).
\]
The first bound is vacuous for $m=1$. For $m=2$ a differing coordinate alone
gives $C(p)$. For $m>16$, the uniform law is a mixture of uniform laws on
16-point subsets, so concavity of output entropy proves the second bound
from $m=16$. If there are $K$ nonempty cells, averaging gives
$I(X;Y\mid U)\ge (7/2-6K/2^n)C(p)\ge2C(p)$. Subtract this from
$I(X;Y)=nC(p)$, using that $U$ is deterministic. The rest of the proof
establishes the finite cell comparisons.

\section{Polarization and finite faces}
Reflect by flipping one bit or by swapping two coordinates $i<j$. The
preferred sides are, respectively, the bit-zero half-cube and the $(1,0)$
side of the swap. Sort each reflected pair of input masses toward the
preferred side. For two points $x,y$ on that side,
$d(x,y)\le d(\sigma x,y)$; hence the binary symmetric transition
probabilities obey $K(x,y)\ge K(\sigma x,y)$. The sum of the two output
masses at $y,\sigma y$ is unchanged. The difference after sorting is a sum
of nonnegative factors $K(x,y)-K(\sigma x,y)$ times absolute input
differences, and therefore dominates the absolute difference before sorting.
A fixed-sum pair of masses has decreasing entropy as its absolute difference
increases. Thus polarization cannot increase output entropy.

For a uniform cell, any nontrivial move decreases the sum of the binary
encodings, and termination gives a shifted downset. A used coordinate
numbered $j$ forces the empty word and at least $j$ singletons. Thus the
support dimension $d$ is at most $m-1$. Fixed coordinates make no net
contribution to $J$. The companion verifier builds sets in increasing word
order, adding masks whose immediate predecessors (remove the lowest bit
when it is bit zero, or shift a bit one position down into a vacancy) are
present. Every stable family satisfies the tested predecessor rules. Any
nonzero mask arises from an immediate predecessor by the reverse move.
Hence this enumeration cannot omit a compressed extremizer. At sizes
3 through 16 it gives counts
\[
 1,2,2,3,4,6,7,10,13,18,23,31,40,54.
\]
For each shape let $t=7/2-6/m$ if $m<16$, and $t=7/2$ if $m=16$.
The comparison is $H(P_A)\ge t\ln2+(d-t)h(p)$, where $P_A$ is the noisy
output law. A four-point coordinate square has $J=2C$ exactly.

\section{Certified entropy comparisons}
\paragraph{High noise.}
Set $\rho=1-2p$ and write the output density as $1+u$ relative to uniform
on the $d$-cube. Every Walsh coefficient of the input density has magnitude
at most one; its nonconstant squared mass is $2^d/m-1$ by Parseval.
The degree-one mass is $B_A=\sum_i(\mathbb E_A(-1)^{X_i})^2$.
After the channel the coefficients of degree $j$ are multiplied by $\rho^j$.
Thus $\lVert u\rVert_\infty\le(1+\rho)^d-1$ and
$\mathbb E u^2\le\rho^2 B_A+\rho^4(2^d/m-1)$. Taylor's theorem applied to
$(1+u)\ln(1+u)$ bounds the divergence from uniform by
\[
 D(P_A\|\mathrm{unif})\le
 \tfrac12\rho^2\frac{B_A+(2^d/m-1)\rho^2}{2-(1+\rho)^d}.
\]
The verifier checks the ratio is at most $d-t$ at $\rho=2^{-15}$ for
each nonexceptional cell. It remains so at smaller $\rho$. The entropy
series gives $C(p)\ge\rho^2/2$, proving the comparison on this end cap.

\paragraph{Low noise.}
The noisy output is a mixture of translates of the uniform cell, so
$H(P_A)\ge\ln m$. The verifier checks this suffices at $p\le2^{-20}$
except for the four-point shapes. For the nonsquare shape, a three-dimensional
star, each point has on average $3/2$ outgoing unit edges. For outputs $y$
outside $A$, $4P_A(y)\le3p$: starting at $y$ the symmetric channel must make
at least one error to reach $A$. The probability of exiting by exactly one
error is $(3/2)p(1-p)^2$. Hence
$H(P_A)-\ln4\ge(3/2)p(1-p)^2(\ln(1/p)-\ln3)>h(p)$
on that cap: use $\ln(1/p)\ge13$, $\ln3<1.1$, and
$h(p)\le p(\ln(1/p)+1)$.

\paragraph{Middle interval.}
Entropy of the noisy output and $h(p)$ both increase with $p$ on
$[0,1/2]$, by the binary channel semigroup and concavity. Thus on a closed
interval $[a,b]$ it is sufficient to certify
$H(P_A(a))>t\ln2+(d-t)h(b)$. At incidence $p=v/D$, $D=2^{40}$, use
$w_y=\sum_{x\in A} v^{d(x,y)}(D-v)^{d-d(x,y)}$ and
$H(P_A)=\ln m+\sum_y w_y\ln(D^d/w_y)/(mD^d)$.
Logarithms are enclosed by extracting powers of two and using 21 terms
of $\ln r=2\operatorname{arctanh}((r-1)/(r+1))$ for $1\le r\le2$;
the positive tail is bounded by $2z^{43}/[43(1-z^2)]$.
All arithmetic for these bounds rounds outwards at scale $2^{80}$.
The supplied script certifies 52,760 dyadic intervals in aggregate,
joining the two end caps. At $p=1/2$ both information quantities are
zero; the noiseless endpoint follows by continuity.
