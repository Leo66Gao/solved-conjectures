# Scope and priority screen, 26 November 2026

**Primary problem and closest prior results**

* Wallman and Flammia, [Randomized Benchmarking with Confidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.6025), Definition 15 and discussion immediately before Proposition 16. The named two-copy conjecture concerns a fixed nonunitary channel under finite exact-design group twirls. The paper did not formally conjecture the quantitative all-orders equality established here.
* Helsen, Wallman, Flammia and Wehner, [Multi-qubit randomized benchmarking using few samples](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.04299), Appendix C, Lemma 5: the unitarity upper-bounds eigenvalues in the traceless-symmetric two-copy Clifford representation. Wallman, Granade, Harper and Flammia, [Estimating the coherence of noise](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/17/11/113020), gives the unitarity and its contraction properties. We do not claim novelty for these ingredients.
* A public [Lean proof of the unrestricted finite-design two-copy result](https://www.trureturing.org/research/wf1404/) explicitly sets tensor powers at least three outside its proved scope. Thus the original two-copy question must NOT be billed as newly solved. No all-orders quantitative equality appears in the public result page.
* Duschenes, García-Martín, Holmes and Cerezo, [Moments of Quantum Channel Ensembles](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12700v2), [PRX Quantum paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/grdp-jph9). It develops the very same ensemble moment operator, block-triangular support decompositions and finite formulas; it gives the two-support eigenvalue e(d²−1)/(d²e²−1), and bounds other cHaar modes asymptotically. Their Theorem 2, Eq. (53), **already gives the exact permutation-sum leading right vector**. Normalized to trace one, this is the familiar tensor moment of the induced Wishart(d,de) law, so that distribution is already implicit at the level of all moments. Our Gaussian marginal coupling gives a short proof and the additional reversibility; the stationary law or moment formula alone should not be advertised as new. The contribution in §3 is not discovery of this individual eigenvalue but identification as the sharp endpoint for all copy numbers and for all L² observables of the reversible stationary process. Their finite Hilbert–Schmidt-norm hierarchy conjecture is a different statement. Its concatenation monotonicity clause was [already refuted](https://www.trureturing.org/research/duschenes-garciamartin-holmes-cerezo-2026-chaar-concatenation/); our results concern spectral decay and do not imply one-step norm monotonicity.
* Movassagh and Schenker, [Theory of Ergodic Quantum Processes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14397), derive stochastic ergodic convergence with particular attention to the independent Haar-isometry example, including moment computations. [An unbiased measure over the matrix product state manifold](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00073), supplement Eqs. (38)–(57), obtains the Marchenko–Pastur shape for this update at large bond dimension (not finite-dimensional exact reversibility or the sharp L² gap). These are direct antecedents, not independent confirmations of novelty.
* Kukulski et al., [Generating random quantum channels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.02994), analyze the fixed-channel ensemble, its unitarity and the output of a pure state. A fixed channel repeatedly applied is different from a sequence of independent channels. Ogura and Martin, [A limit formula for joint spectral radius with p-radius of probability distributions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3026), provide the classical random-product/Kronecker-moment background. The moment comparison for bounded products in §1 is elementary in this context; no new random-matrix machinery is claimed.
* [Exact spectral gaps of random one-dimensional quantum circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11201) investigates the second unitary moment. [Conditional t-independent spectral gap for random quantum circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13739) treats the richer unitary-moment representation and three-site operators. Our §4 is a statement on the entire **projective pure-state** space and hence all projective-state moments, not a solution of an all-orders unitary-design gap problem.

**Search coverage and uncertainty**

Searches combined the older authors' names with “n-contractive”, “all moments”, “three-copy”, “unitarity”, “higher tensor powers”; searched “quantum channel ensemble moment operator”, “spectral gap”, “cHaar”, “Haar isometry”, “reversible”, “stationary Wishart”, “right environment”, “exact stationary distribution”, “maximal correlation” of random quantum marginals, and three-site projective-state circuit gaps. Public AI records were checked; they already cover the two-copy Wallman–Flammia statement and cHaar norm counterexample. The user's existing repository overview had no match for this research line. No identified source states the general exact two-copy spectral stabilization together with the exact Haar-Stinespring L² gap and equilibrium proof. This is not an exhaustive database search or outside-specialist priority review. In particular, variants of the elementary bounded-product lemma and Wishart invariance could be known under names in stochastic systems, random matrix-product states, correlation operators, or multivariate probability. The safe description is **a proved all-orders extension and exact rate, with reasonable but unconfirmed originality**, not a claim to be the first.

**Reproducibility** Run `python3 check.py` with SymPy. It independently projects the Haar average onto the permutation commutant using its exact Gram matrix. It verifies the subdominant eigenvalue and stationary tensor across four (d,e) pairs at orders 2,3,4, plus two nonunital Clifford examples with opposite choices of dominant sector. The universal statements follow from proof.md, not finite extrapolation.

Section 5's sharp maximal-correlation statement for unequal Haar end marginals also has a separate exact rederivation of the *finite tests*, using pure-state Haar permutation moments and the Gram matrices of traces of the second and third powers of each marginal. Run `python3 check_marginals.py`. All five tests, including (a,e,b)=(12,2,2) and (9,2,3) where a>eb, agree with the theoretical extremum. As before, these checks do not replace the proof over arbitrary square-integrable observables.
